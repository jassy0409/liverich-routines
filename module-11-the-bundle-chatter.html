What this module covers

Bundles are one of the most powerful tools in your arsenal — when used right. A well-timed bundle offer can triple a single transaction. This module teaches you when to pitch a bundle, how to frame it, and how to make the fan feel like he's getting a deal without ever discounting.

Key principles

  • 1When is the best time to pitch a bundle?
    A bundle pitch lands best when trust is established. One purchase + enthusiasm = ready for the bundle conversation.
    Example: First purchase done → fan is warm → introduce bundle as a natural next step.
  • 2How should you frame a bundle to make it feel like value, not a pitch?
    Personalisation makes bundles irresistible. 'I put this together for you' beats 'here's what's included' every time.
    Example: "okay so i actually put something together that made me think of you specifically 👀"
  • 3Fan bought a $30 PPV. Which bundle pitch timing is right?
    Let him enjoy the first purchase. Follow up in the next session when he's already happy — that's when he's most open to more.
    Example: Let him watch → follow up next session → mention the bundle naturally.
  • 4Fan says 'that's a lot of money.' How do you respond to a bundle objection?
    Never discount. Instead, reframe the value. Help him see what he's getting, not what he's spending.
    Example: "i get it — but think about it: this is [X], [Y], and [Z] all in one... honestly it's the best deal on my page 🖤"
  • 5What's the maximum number of items in a well-structured bundle?
    2–4 items with a theme feels curated and intentional. More than that feels like a dump. Less than 2 isn't really a bundle.
    Example: 2-4 items, same theme, presented as a curated collection — never a random mix.
