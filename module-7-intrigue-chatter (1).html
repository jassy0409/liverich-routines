The Intrigue Stage

The fan hasn't decided yet. Your job in this stage is to make him curious enough to stay — without giving everything away. Intrigue is the art of the open door.

What is the IRAE framework?

IRAE is the emotional journey every fan goes through before he becomes a loyal, repeat buyer. Intrigue → Rapport → Attraction → Enslavement. Each stage has a different goal. You can't skip stages — trying to sell to a fan who's still in Intrigue is like proposing on a first date. Learn where he is, and work the right stage.

What is Intrigue?

The fan just arrived. He's a stranger. He doesn't know if she's worth his time or money yet. Intrigue is the stage where you create just enough mystery to make him want to find out more.

The core principle

Don't explain — imply.

Most chatters over-explain. They describe what the content looks like, what's in it, how long it is. That's a product listing, not a conversation. In the Intrigue stage, your job is to hint at something he hasn't seen yet — and let his imagination fill in the rest. His imagination will always do a better job than your description.

🎯 Rule — leave a gap. If you've said everything, there's no reason for him to stay.

Why Intrigue comes first

Attention is earned before money is spent.

A fan who is not intrigued will not engage in Discovery. A fan who skips Discovery never reaches the Sale. Every dollar you earn from a fan was preceded by a moment where he thought, "I want to know more." Intrigue creates that moment.

Fan · new subscriberhey
✕ Skipping Intriguehi! i just posted a new video, want to see it? it's $25
✓ Intrigue firsthey 👀 i was literally just about to film something i've never done before. your timing is either perfect or very dangerous lol

The second message creates a story. He wants to know what she's filming. Now you have his attention.

The 4 Intrigue tools

These are the four moves that reliably create curiosity. Learn each one so you can reach for the right one in any conversation.

Tool 1 · The Unfinished Story

Start something. Don't finish it.

A story that ends closes the loop — he's satisfied and has no reason to respond. A story that pauses mid-sentence forces him to ask what happens next. Use this to open conversations and to re-engage cold fans.

✓ Unfinished story openersomething unexpected happened on my shoot today and i honestly don't know whether to laugh or be embarrassed about it 😅
Fanlol what happened?? 😂

🎯 He asked. You didn't push — you pulled. That's the difference between Intrigue and selling.

Tool 2 · The Tease Without a Reveal

Mention the thing. Don't show the thing.

Reference content, a look, a feeling, a situation — but don't fully describe it. Leave one detail out. The missing detail is what makes him curious.

✓ Tease without reveali filmed something last night that i'm honestly a little nervous to post… it's a different side of me than what you've seen 🙈
Fandifferent how? now i need to know

🎯 "A different side" is the gap. He fills it with his best fantasy — and now he's invested.

Tool 3 · Selective Access

Make him feel like he almost missed something.

Exclusivity is a powerful intrigue trigger. When a fan feels like access to something is limited — by time, by who gets to see it, by what it takes to unlock it — his interest sharpens. This is not fake urgency. It's reflecting real scarcity that already exists on the page.

✓ Selective accessi only sent this to the guys who've been around a while because honestly it's a little too real for a mass send 🙈 you're on that list though
Fanoh yeah? what is it

🎯 He feels chosen, not marketed to. That's a completely different emotional state.

Tool 4 · The Callback Question

Reference something from his past and ask forward.

Nothing creates intrigue in a returning fan like proof that she remembers him. A callback question — referencing something from a previous conversation, then asking a forward question — makes him feel seen. A fan who feels seen stays curious about what comes next.

✓ Callback questionhey, didn't you tell me your birthday was coming up? 👀 because i may or may not have something in mind
Fanwait yeah it's next week lol what are you thinking 😏

🎯 He's leaning forward now. That's the posture you want before a sale.

When Intrigue goes wrong

Two mistakes that kill the stage before it can work.

  • ✕Over-teasing. If you tease and never deliver, fans learn to ignore the hints. Intrigue has to eventually lead somewhere real. The rule: every tease earns the right to make one offer — after Rapport is established.
  • ✕Skipping to the sell. Sending a PPV link in the first or second message to a cold fan is the single most common chatter error. He doesn't know her yet. He has no emotional investment. Of course he says no. You haven't done the work.
  • ✕Generic openers. "Hey how are you 😊" opens no loop. It creates no story. There is nothing to be curious about. Every opener should have at least one piece of information that invites a follow-up question from him.

Golden rules of Intrigue

  • ★Your job is to make him curious — not to make the sale. The sale comes later.
  • ★Every message should open a loop or close one that was already open.
  • ★His imagination is your best tool. Give it something to work with, then step back.
  • ★Never send a PPV to a fan who hasn't shown curiosity or engagement first.
  • ★Intrigue is over when he starts asking questions. That's when Rapport begins.
