What this module covers

A fan who went quiet is not a lost fan. They're a fan waiting for the right reason to come back. This module teaches you the exact re-entry plays for fans who ghosted, expired, or just drifted — and how to bring them back without coming across as desperate.

Key principles

  • 1A fan spent $200 last month and hasn't messaged in 3 weeks. Your first move?
    High-value fans who go quiet need to feel remembered, not sold to. Personal check-in first, pitch much later.
    Example: "hey — been thinking about you. did that thing with work ever calm down? 🖤"
  • 2Fan's subscription expired and he hasn't renewed. What do you do?
    An expired fan chose to leave — but a warm, personal message can remind him why he was here. No pressure, just connection.
    Example: "i noticed you've been gone and honestly just wanted to check in on you 🥺 everything okay?"
  • 3Fan ghosted after a PPV rejection. How long do you wait before reaching out?
    Give the rejection time to settle. 2–5 days is long enough for the fan to move on from the 'no' and be open again.
    Example: Wait 2-5 days, then re-enter with warmth and zero mention of the PPV rejection.
  • 4What should you NEVER do when rekindling a fan?
    Pointing out absence or lack of spending makes fans feel guilty and defensive. Come back with warmth, never with pressure.
    Example: Never say: "you haven't been around" or "you haven't bought anything lately."
  • 5Best rekindle opener for a fan you haven't heard from in 2 weeks?
    Specific personal references feel genuine. Generic 'miss you' messages feel like copy-paste blasts.
    Example: "hey you — i randomly thought about that thing you mentioned about [topic] 🥺 how did that go?"
