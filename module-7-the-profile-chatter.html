The Profile

What a fan profile actually is

  • 1It's not a spreadsheet — it's a memory. A profile is everything you know about a fan stored in your head (or your notes): what he loves, what he's bought, what made him tip, what he mentioned once that you remembered.
  • 2The goal is relevance. A message that speaks directly to him — his interests, his mood, his history — converts at 3–4x the rate of a generic caption. Every fan wants to feel like she knows him.
  • 3You build it in real time. Every conversation is a data point. He mentioned his dog? Note it. He bought fitness content twice? Note it. He tips when she calls him by name? Note it.
  • 4Notes are a team asset. Your profile notes pass to the next chatter. If you don't write it down, that connection disappears at shift change.

The memory play — your most powerful tool

The memory play is simple: you remember something personal the fan told you, and you open with it the next time he messages.

  • 1Before anything else. The memory comes first — before the pitch, before the PPV, before anything. "How did that job interview go?" lands before "I have something for you."
  • 2It doesn't have to be big. He mentioned he likes basketball. Open with "did you watch the game last night?" That's it. He feels seen. The rest is easier.
  • 3Write it in the fan note immediately. Don't trust yourself to remember 6 hours later. Note it the second the conversation ends.

Fan: "I'm a nurse, crazy week"

Next session opener:

"you still surviving out there? nurses don't get enough credit honestly 🥺 I was thinking about you"

He didn't expect her to remember. That surprise is the whole game.

Fan tiers — warm, regular, whale

  • 1Warm fan: Subscribed, chatted, maybe tipped small. Building phase — your job is discovery. Learn what he likes before you pitch anything big.
  • 2Regular: Has bought 2–3 times, responds to messages. Trust is established. You can escalate content and price with confidence here.
  • 3Whale: $500+ lifetime, has a pattern. He has specific triggers — find them and speak to them every time. Do NOT treat him like a warm fan. He earns personal, personalised attention every session.

The mistake most chatters make: treating all three tiers the same way. A warm fan getting a $200 pitch will leave. A whale getting a generic opener will feel disrespected.

Re-engaging a quiet fan

A fan who went quiet is not a lost fan — they're a fan waiting for the right reason to come back. The wrong move is a cold pitch. The right move is a warm re-entry.

  • 1Reference something personal first. "Been thinking about you — did that thing with your sister ever get sorted?" Zero pitch. Just connection.
  • 2Give him something easy to respond to. A question, a light tease, something that doesn't require him to spend. He needs to feel safe re-entering.
  • 3Wait for warmth, then pitch. Once he's talking again, the door is open. Don't rush it. One warm exchange is worth more than three cold pitches.

When to send a big-ticket PPV

  • 1Earn the right first. Tip received → warm chat → small content win → relationship = now you can send the big ask. Skip any step and the answer is usually no.
  • 2Make it personal. "I was thinking about you when I shot this — you mentioned you like [X], this is exactly that" converts at 2x a generic caption.
  • 3One ask, full confidence. Send it once with a personalised caption. If he says no, go to The Chase module — don't repeat the same pitch.

Golden rules of the profile

  • 1Note everything personal — job, pets, hobbies, what made him tip last time.
  • 2Open with memory before you open with a pitch — every single time.
  • 3Match your pitch to his tier — warm, regular, or whale.
  • 4Re-engage quiet fans with warmth, not product.
  • 5Your notes are the next chatter's starting point — write them like you're handing off a VIP guest.
